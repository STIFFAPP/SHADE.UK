ENGINEERING & SAFETY

Designed for the site.
Not guessed on site.

A canopy has to resist wind, uplift, snow and its own loads while controlling membrane shape, drainage and movement. That is why final structural design sits between survey and fabrication.

DESIGN PROCESS

What gets checked.

Loads & exposure

Site wind exposure, relevant snow loading, geometry, drainage and ponding risk.

Steel & connections

Member sizing, deflection, base plates, gussets, bolts, welds and modular joints where applicable.

Ground & anchors

Foundation reactions, anchors, edge distances and foundation design based on the actual site information.

Membrane

Three-dimensional tensile form, patterning, attachment details and controlled pretension rather than a simple flat sheet.

Fabrication

Approved drawings are translated into fabrication information with material grades, connection details and tolerances.

Installation

Set-out, lifting, work-at-height, temporary stability, fixing and tensioning sequence are planned before work starts.

CONSTRUCTION PRINCIPLES

Durable by design.

Steel protection

Development direction: hot-dip galvanised steel plus exterior powder coating for a durable architectural finish.

Replaceable membrane

The keder/luff-track concept is intended to allow the membrane skin to be replaced without replacing the whole frame.

Drainage

Water management is treated as part of the canopy geometry, not an afterthought.

Any dimensions shown in concept artwork are development information only. They are not approved fabrication dimensions, foundation instructions or universal structural specifications.
